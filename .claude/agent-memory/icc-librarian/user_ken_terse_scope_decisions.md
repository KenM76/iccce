Ken decides scope in very few words. On 2026-08-11 an enumerated list of
four open questions came back as, in full: **"1 is done. 2. do all."** —
which was expanded into three decision-log entries and a README scope
reversal.

**How to apply when filing his decisions:**

- **Quote him verbatim, and quote the question he was answering**, so a
  reader can re-derive the expansion. A terse instruction turned into
  paragraphs of rationale is exactly where a librarian either labels the
  expansion or launders it into the record as though he had said it.
- **Attribute nothing to him that he did not say.** He typically supplies
  no depth, priority, schedule or per-item rationale. Any reasoning in
  the filing is the project's own and is revisable without going back to
  him.
- **A terse yes is not a blanket authorisation.** "Do all" answered a
  three-item list; it is not a go-ahead to push, tag, release or publish.
  Under global rule 9 those still need an explicit *current* go-ahead,
  and a recorded *intent* to publish is precisely the artefact a future
  agent could misread as standing approval.
- He is fine with the heavy documentation treatment of a one-line answer
  — the expansion is wanted, the mislabelling is not.

# iccce — licensing and sourcing

## 1. This project

**MIT**, matching `pdfce` so the two compose without friction. Every
dependency must be permissive; copyleft is flagged to the operator and
never adopted unilaterally. `THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES.md` is generated by
`cargo-about`, never hand-written.

## 2. The ICC specifications

The ICC publishes ICC.1 and ICC.2 for free download. **"Freely
available" is not "freely redistributable"** — before any bulk
extraction into the RAG, read the actual terms on the ICC's site and
record here what they permit, with the date checked.

That has been done (§2.1). The standing corpus rule is: **summarise and
cite, transcribe only what is needed to implement, and keep the corpus
private and uncommitted.** This rule is **unchanged** by the operator
retrieval recorded in §2.4 — acquisition is not a reproduction licence.

**Verdict on redistribution: the fallback rule in §2 stands, unchanged
and now evidenced rather than assumed.** The ToS grants no reproduction
right. It does not carve out an exception for technical implementation,
and the licence-like permission notice that some standards bodies attach
to a freely-downloadable specification **is absent here** — there is no
"may be copied and distributed subject to…" grant anywhere on the specs
page, the ToS, or the IP policy. The IP Policy page is **patent-only**
(it adopts the ITU-T/ITU-R/ISO/IEC Common Patent Policy guidelines of
23 April 2012) and says nothing about copyright or reproduction.

Operative corpus rule, therefore:

- **Summarise and cite.** Short verbatim quotation with a clause
  citation is normal technical-reference practice and is what the corpus
  does; bulk verbatim transcription of clause text is not.
- **Transcribe only what implementation needs** — field offsets,
  encodings, formulas. Facts and numbers are not copyrightable
  expression (§5); the prose around them is.
- **The corpus stays private and uncommitted.** Note the ToS phrase
  *"any other … networked computer environment"* is broad: `ICC_Spec`
  must stay on local disk. **Do not place it on `R:\` (Dropbox-synced)
  or any network share**, and never in a git remote.

### 2.2 ★ BLOCKER (CLEARED 2026-08-11 by operator retrieval — see §2.4) — automated retrieval is prohibited by the ToS (OPERATOR DECISION)

**Status: the blocker described below was resolved on 2026-08-11 by
option (1), the operator's own browser download. §2.2 is retained
unedited as the dated record of why no agent fetched the document, and
the prohibition on *agent* retrieval from `color.org` remains in force
permanently.**

**This is a hard gate and it was not worked around.** The ToS
**USE OF SERVICES** section prohibits, verbatim:

> "using **any robot, spider, or other automated device to access the
> Services for any purpose, including monitoring, copying, or training
> artificial intelligence or machine learning models, without prior
> written consent from ICC**"

An LLM agent fetching with `curl`/WebFetch is squarely within that
clause — and the clause names AI/ML training explicitly, so this is not
a strained reading. **`icc-spec-librarian` therefore did NOT download
`ICC.1-2022-05.pdf` or any other ICC-hosted specification PDF.** The
three pages listed in §2.1 were fetched to *determine the terms*; on
reading them, retrieval stopped there.

**The conflicting signal, recorded because the disagreement is the
finding:** `www.color.org/robots.txt` — the machine-readable statement
of what automated agents may do, and the convention an agent is normally
expected to honour — permits everything except `/accounts/`,
`/dashboard/` and `/documents/`. It does **not** disallow the
specification index. `archive.color.org` serves no `robots.txt` at all
(HTTP 404, a Cloudflare R2 bucket). So the site's machine-readable
permission and its prose contract point opposite ways. **The prose
contract is the one that binds, so the restrictive reading was taken.**

**What would settle it, in order of cost:**

1. **Ken downloads the PDF himself in a browser** — a human retrieval,
   entirely outside the robot clause — and drops it at
   `D:\Dev\Rag-Specialized\ICC_Spec\_sources\ICC.1-2022-05.pdf`. The
   corpus can then be built against it under the §2.1 rules. **This is
   the cheap answer and the recommended one.**
2. Written consent from ICC for automated access (`info@color.org`).
3. Confirmation that ICC intends the robot clause to apply only to
   scraping/abuse rather than to retrieving a document it publishes for
   free. Plausible, but not something a librarian may assume.

Until (1) or (2), **no ICC-hosted document is a source for this corpus**,
and every file built in the meantime says so in its frontmatter.

### 2.3 The sourcing route actually used, and its evidence tier

With the primary blocked, Tier 1 was built from two **permissively
licensed, independently maintained** sources — deliberately two, so that
every load-bearing constant is cross-verified rather than asserted:

| Source | Licence | Verified | Standing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`icProfileHeader.h`**, ICC's own `DemoIccMAX` / iccDEV project, GitHub `InternationalColorConsortium/DemoIccMAX` | **BSD-3-Clause**, "Copyright (c) International Color Consortium" (`LICENSE.md`, retrieved 2026-08-11) | 2026-08-11 | **ICC-authored secondary.** Its own header comment states it "corresponds to the profile specification version 4.3 as defined in ICC Specificion ICC.1:2010" and that "**Conflicts between this header file and the ICC profile specification (if they exist) should be deferred to the ICC profile specification.**" It is authoritative-adjacent, **not** the standard. |
| **`lcms2.h`**, Little-CMS 2.19 (`LCMS_VERSION 2190`), GitHub `mm2/Little-CMS` | **MIT**, "Copyright (c) 2023 Marti Maria Saguer" (`LICENSE`, retrieved 2026-08-11) | 2026-08-11 | Independent implementation. Cross-check only — per project rule 7, disagreement with lcms2 is a finding, not a verdict. |

Both are **source code under permissive licences that expressly permit
use and redistribution**, so they carry none of §2.1's constraints. They
are also *not* "the Services" — GitHub is not ICC-operated — so §2.2's
robot clause does not reach them.

**The cost of this route, stated plainly:** a C header encodes
signatures, offsets and enumerations exactly, and encodes **prose
requirements not at all**. Everything normative — which tags are
*required* per profile class, what a decoder *shall* do with an
out-of-range value, the rendering-intent semantics, the interpolation
rule between LUT grid points — **is not recoverable from this route**
and is recorded as an open gap, not guessed. This is why §2.2 item (1)
is worth Ken's two minutes.

### 2.4 ★ Option (1) exercised — operator retrieval, 2026-08-11

**Ken retrieved `ICC.1-2022-05.pdf` himself, in a browser, on
2026-08-11**, and placed it at
`D:\Dev\Rag-Specialized\ICC_Spec\_sources\ICC.1-2022-05.pdf`
(905 961 bytes; file mtime 2026-08-11 11:12). PDF metadata, read
locally: `/Title` "ICC.1-2202-05.pdf" *(sic — the ICC's own metadata
transposes 2022 to 2202)*, `/Author` "International Color Consortium",
`/CreationDate` 2022-04-25, 126 pages. The document identifies itself
as **ICC.1:2022**, profile version **4.4.0.0**.

**Why this is outside §2.2's prohibition:** the ToS clause bars "any
robot, spider, or other automated device" from accessing the Services.
A person clicking a download link in a browser is none of those. **No
agent fetched this file, and none may** — §2.2's rule against agent
retrieval from `color.org` / `archive.color.org` stands unchanged and
permanently.

**What this changes:** the corpus may now cite ICC.1:2022 as
**`primary_spec`**, with real clause, table and equation numbers. The
`icc-spec-librarian` corpus was upgraded against it on 2026-08-11.

**What this does NOT change — read before quoting anything outward:**

1. **§2.1 governs reproduction, and §2.1 is unchanged.** The ToS grants
   no reproduction right; possessing a copy is not a licence to
   redistribute one. **Summarise-and-cite still applies to every piece
   of text that leaves the corpus** — including into source comments,
   `docs/`, commit messages, issues, or any published artifact. Short
   verbatim quotation with a clause citation is normal technical
   practice and is permitted; bulk verbatim clause text is not.
2. **The corpus stays private and uncommitted**, on local `D:\` only.
   Not `R:\` (Dropbox-synced), not any share, not any git remote — the
   ToS bars reproduction "on any other web site or networked computer
   environment".
3. **The `_sources\` PDF itself is never committed** and never copied
   out of `D:\Dev\Rag-Specialized\ICC_Spec\_sources\`.
4. **Facts are still facts.** Field offsets, encodings, constants and
   equations are not copyrightable expression (§5) and may be
   transcribed into iccce freely, with a clause citation. The prose
   around them may not.

**Tool limitation encountered and reported rather than worked around:**
the agent's PDF *page-image* reader requires `pdftoppm` (poppler), which
is not installed for it, so no page was read as an image. Text
extraction was performed locally with **three independent engines** —
`pypdf` 6.7.0, poppler `pdftotext -layout`, and `pdfminer.six` — and
every load-bearing passage was extracted by at least two of them and
compared. **One systematic extraction hazard was found and is recorded
in the corpus:** ICC.1:2022 sets mathematical signs in the Symbol font,
which all three engines render into the Unicode private-use area
(`U+F02D` minus, `U+F02B` plus). Naïve extraction therefore **silently
drops the minus sign**, turning "−128,0" into "128,0". Any future
extraction from this PDF must map the private-use range before reading
numbers out of it.

## 3. Profiles as test fixtures

**A profile is a copyrighted work.** Most vendor profiles ship under
licences that do not permit redistribution, and a repository is
redistribution.

Three categories:

- **(a) Synthetic** — authored byte by byte by this project's own
  generator. Unrestricted. **Prefer these for everything.**
- **(b) Explicitly free** — profiles published under terms that permit
  redistribution (some ICC-published and some open-source ones qualify).
  Each carries its source and licence in `fixtures/reference/PROVENANCE.md`.
- **(c) Everything else** — including anything from a Windows or Adobe
  installation. **Never committed.** Tests may read them from the local
  system and skip when absent; they must never be a required input.

The sibling project's rule applies unchanged: a fixture of unknown
provenance is not a fixture.

### 4.2 ⚠ The finding: lcms2 is NOT uniformly MIT

**This is the part a licence badge does not tell you, and it is the
reason §4 asked for verification rather than assumption.**

Three files in the pinned tree carry licences other than MIT:

| Path | Licence | Reaches us? |
|---|---|---|
| `plugins/fast_float/` (`COPYING.GPL3`, and a GPL-3.0-or-later notice in every source file) | **GPL-3.0-or-later** | **No** — not built |
| `plugins/threaded/` (`COPYING.GPL3`, same notice) | **GPL-3.0-or-later** | **No** — not built |
| `utils/jpgicc/iccjpeg.c` (`LICENSE_iccjpeg`) | **IJG** (Independent JPEG Group; permissive with an acknowledgement requirement) | **No** — `jpgicc` not built |

Upstream states this itself, in `plugins/README.1ST`, verbatim:

> "IMPORTANT: Before adding those plug-ins to your commercial project,
> please check licenses for each plugin.
>
> LittleCMS core is released under MIT, but plug-ins may be released
> under other license. fast_float and threaded are GPL3"

The GPL-3.0 notice in those plugin sources reads in full: *"This program
is free software: you can redistribute it and/or modify it under the
terms of the GNU General Public License as published by the Free Software
Foundation, either version 3 of the License, or (at your option) any
later version."*

**How this project is insulated, in three independent layers** — because
one layer is a setting somebody can flip, and this is a constraint that
should survive carelessness:

1. **We do not link lcms2 at all.** The oracle is invoked as a *separate
   process* (`transicc.exe`), out-of-tree, from a test harness. No iccce
   crate has lcms2 as a dependency, in any profile, dev or otherwise.
   `ARCHITECTURE.md` §1 and ROADMAP Pass 0 require this for
   independence-of-oracle reasons; it happens to also be the strongest
   licence firewall available.
2. **We do not build the GPL plugins.** `build-lcms2.ps1` and
   `build-lcms2.sh` both pass `-DLCMS2_WITH_FASTFLOAT=OFF
   -DLCMS2_WITH_THREADED_PLUGIN=OFF`. Upstream's `cmake/Lcms2Options.cmake`
   already defaults both to `OFF`; we set them explicitly so the intent is
   recorded rather than inherited, and so a future upstream default change
   cannot alter what we build.
3. **We do not carry the source.** `tools/difftest/vendor/` is
   git-ignored; `fetch-lcms2.sh` clones on demand. GPL-3.0 source never
   enters this repository's tree or its history — which is a matter of
   fact rather than a matter of a build flag.

**There is a second, non-legal reason `fast_float` must stay off**, and
it belongs in the record because it would otherwise look like a purely
legal choice that a future engineer might trade away: `fast_float`
substitutes an approximate floating-point pipeline for lcms2's careful
one. An oracle must be the reference implementation's *most accurate*
path. Comparing iccce against an approximation would make every
disagreement ambiguous — was it our error, or the plugin's? — which
defeats the purpose of having an oracle at all.

### 4.3 Standing obligation when the pin moves

**Moving the pin is a licence event, not a version bump.** lcms2's
licensing is per-directory and upstream has changed the plugin set
before. On any change to `tools/difftest/lcms2.pin`:

1. Re-read the top-level `LICENSE` at the new commit.
2. Re-enumerate every `LICENSE*` / `COPYING*` file in the tree
   (`find . -iname 'LICENSE*' -o -iname 'COPYING*'`) — that is how the
   GPL-3.0 plugins were found.
3. Re-check `plugins/README.1ST` for added plugins.
4. Append a new dated subsection here. Do not edit §4.1 in place; a
   verification is a dated observation and stays as one.

### 4.4 What is *not* claimed

- This is a reading of the licence files as they stand at the pinned
  commit. It is not legal advice and no lawyer reviewed it.
- No audit was made of lcms2's own third-party inputs beyond the
  `LICENSE`/`COPYING` sweep and the per-file header spot-checks named
  above. That sweep found three non-MIT items and no others; it is a
  file-name-driven sweep, not a line-by-line provenance audit of 60k
  lines of C.
- Nothing here licenses iccce to *ship* lcms2. We ship no part of it.

## 5. Reference values

Published CIE and vendor numbers are facts, not creative works, and
citing them with a source is fine. Transcribe the source alongside the
value — a number without a citation is indistinguishable from one
somebody produced and liked.
